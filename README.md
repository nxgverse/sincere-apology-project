# Echoes of Us

Build a complete private apology website for my ex-girlfriend, Zipporah.



IMPORTANT: This is a bespoke emotional experience, NOT a generic romantic website, Valentine template, wedding site, AI love-letter template, or normal landing page.



VISUAL QUALITY: The design and polish should feel like Apple.com/ng. Do NOT copy Apple branding, layouts, logos or assets. Match the level of craftsmanship: exceptional typography, huge intentional whitespace, cinematic composition, refined spacing, premium responsive design, subtle motion, excellent media presentation and obsessive attention to detail.



Think: Apple product page + cinematic short film + private digital love letter.



DESIGN:

Use a dark near-black environment, sophisticated modern sans-serif typography, off-white text, restrained borders, subtle shadows, large editorial typography, generous whitespace and premium rounded media containers. Use fluid responsive typography and carefully art-directed mobile layouts.



NO flowers, roses, floating hearts, glitter, confetti, pink Valentine aesthetics, cursive fonts, stock romantic imagery, cheesy gradients, excessive particles, excessive parallax, generic card grids, generic loading spinners, generic navbar or template-looking UI.



The website should feel expensive, quiet, intimate and custom-built for one person.



OPENING:

Start with a full-screen cinematic intro. Everything is heavily blurred (roughly 20–30px), slightly scaled up (~1.03), and partially transparent.



Show:



“Hey, you.”



“Before you go any further, I need you to let me say this.”



Button: “Enter”



When Enter is tapped:



- Start the attached AEO audio.

- Fade the audio in smoothly.

- Resolve the blur to 0.

- Fade content to full opacity.

- Scale from ~1.03 to 1.

- Transition into the main experience.



Do not use a normal loading spinner.



AUDIO:

Use the attached AEO audio file as the background soundtrack. Do not source or replace it with another song.



Add a subtle custom floating mute/unmute control. Do not use the browser's default audio player.



VIDEOS ARE A CORE PART OF THE EXPERIENCE:

I have attached personal videos of me and Zipporah from the last time we were together.



These are REAL memories.



Do NOT treat them as a gallery.



Do NOT put all the videos into a generic grid.



Instead, weave them into the “Our Memories” portion of the story as cinematic visual chapters. The written story should occasionally stop and allow a real memory to breathe.



Think:



story → silence → memory → reflection → story → memory



Use large cinematic video containers with 20–32px rounded corners, subtle borders/shadows, beautiful crops and generous whitespace.



Create custom premium video controls:



- play/pause

- progress

- mute/unmute

- fullscreen where appropriate

- controls fade away when inactive

- subtle translucent play button when paused



Videos must NOT autoplay with sound.



Lazy-load videos, use poster frames where possible, and pause distant videos.



Use subtle blur/opacity/scale entrance animations.



AEO + VIDEO AUDIO:

When a video with meaningful audio begins playing, smoothly lower the AEO soundtrack. When the video pauses or ends, smoothly restore the soundtrack. Never abruptly switch audio.

- anything that is not clearly visible or reliably known



If chronology is reliably available from filenames/metadata, preserve it. Otherwise use the provided/upload order.



Use neutral captions when necessary.

THE STORY:



I was the one who ended the relationship.



Zipporah did not leave me. She did not decide we weren't worth it. She did not stop caring. I made the decision.



The reason I gave myself was distance. Being apart felt difficult, and I convinced myself that difficult meant impossible. I now understand those are not the same thing. Distance is hard, missing someone is hard, and not being able to see someone whenever you want is hard, but I made the mistake of treating difficulty like a conclusion.



I was wrong, and I regret it deeply.



After the breakup, I acted nonchalant and distant. This was not because I stopped caring. I didn't know how to be “just friends” with her.



I thought that if I became distant enough, stopped talking as much, stopped looking for reasons to interact with her and acted like everything was normal, eventually the feelings would disappear.



They didn't.



I was hurting quietly.



My distance may have looked like indifference, but it wasn't. I was distant because I cared too much and didn't know how to exist around her without wanting her back.



I haven't been able to function properly without her in my life.



I've continued working, studying, building things, laughing, talking to people and doing everything I'm supposed to do, but underneath everything there has been an emptiness because she is no longer part of my everyday life.



I miss the conversations.



The random moments.



The things I would normally tell her.



Being able to have someone on the other side of my phone who was her.



I didn't realize how deeply she had become part of my everyday life until I deliberately removed her from it.



I don't want a life where I simply have to get used to her not being in it.



I miss her.



Not an imaginary version of her.



Her.



I miss her voice, our conversations, the stupid little things that probably didn't seem important at the time, laughing together, being able to tell her random things and knowing she'd understand why I was telling her.



I miss being us.



I could make excuses, but I don't want to.



I made the decision.



I have to own it.



She didn't deserve to be left wondering whether she was worth the effort.



She didn't deserve to have someone who loved her decide that the circumstances were enough reason to walk away.



And she didn't deserve my pretending afterward that I was fine.



I'm sorry.



Genuinely sorry.



I now understand that loving someone doesn't mean everything will always be easy.



Sometimes loving someone means looking at something difficult and asking:



“How do we figure this out?”



Instead, I looked at the distance and decided the answer for both of us.



I didn't give us the chance to figure it out together.



That's what I regret.



The problem wasn't that I stopped loving her.



The problem was that I convinced myself love wasn't enough to make the difficult parts worth facing.



If I could go back to the moment I ended things, I wouldn't do it.



I'd talk to her.



I'd tell her I was scared.



I'd tell her the distance was getting to me.



I'd tell her I didn't know how we'd make it work.



And then I'd ask how we could figure it out together instead of deciding that we should end it.



I know I hurt her.



I know an apology doesn't erase what happened.



She may be angry.



She may be confused.



She may not trust me the same way anymore.



She may need time.



She may not even want the relationship anymore.



I don't get to decide how she feels about what happened.



I will not pressure her.



I will not ask her to forget.



I will not ask her to pretend nothing happened.



I simply want her to know that I understand what I did now and that I regret it.



The thing I've been too scared to say is simple:



I want her back.



I still love her.



I don't want to lose her because I was foolish enough to think distance was the end of our story.



I am not asking for a perfect relationship.



Distance will still be difficult.



There will still be difficult days.



There will still be moments when being apart sucks.



But if there is still something here, I'd rather face those things with her than lose her because I was afraid of them.



I want another chance.



Not to pretend nothing happened.



But to do better.



To communicate better.



To be honest when something is bothering me instead of running from it.



To stop treating obstacles like conclusions.



To actually fight for something before deciding it is impossible.



The website should eventually ask:



“Can we try again?”



The request must feel sincere, accountable and respectful — never manipulative.
